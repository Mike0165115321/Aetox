# What should we get written?

- A quote you can send a client as it is | Write me a quote. Ask me first for all of it: what is being sold, how many, the unit price, who the client is and their address, whether VAT applies, and how long the price holds. For any number I have not given you, do not fill in a sample — leave it blank and tell me what is still missing. Work out the totals and the tax, then check the arithmetic a second time. Hand it back as a .docx that prints without shifting, and tell me what else the document would need if the client wants to file it for reimbursement. | fileText
- A full-form tax invoice that holds up | Write me a full-form tax invoice. Open the tax-invoice skill first and read what Thai law requires the document to carry, then ask me for every required field I have not supplied, especially both parties' taxpayer numbers and the registered addresses. Do not leave a mandatory field empty and move on. Show the pre-tax amount, the VAT, and the total as separate lines, and check the rounding again. Hand it back as a .docx, then tell me which fields the buyer will use to reclaim input VAT and what goes wrong if one of them is filled in incorrectly. | layoutList
- Minutes, written from the recording | Turn this recording into meeting minutes. Transcribe the whole file first — do not summarise from the opening minutes. Where the audio is unclear, mark that spot rather than guessing at the word. Then split it three ways: what was discussed, what was actually agreed, and what somebody now has to do, with the owner and the deadline where one was said aloud. Keep anything discussed but left unresolved in its own section — do not write it up as though it were settled. Hand it back as a .docx with the timestamp in the recording against each heading. | headphones
- A contract draft with the clauses that matter | Draft this contract for me. Ask me first who the parties are, what is being agreed, for how long, what is paid and when, what counts as delivered, who owns the work, and what happens if it ends early. For anything I do not answer, put in the standard option and flag it as something I must read and decide myself — do not quietly swallow it. Write it in language somebody who is not a lawyer can follow. Hand it back as a .docx, close with the three clauses that carry the most risk for me, and state plainly that this is a draft for a lawyer to review, not legal advice. | shield
- What this document is missing before it goes out | Review this document before I send it. Read the whole thing before you start commenting. Check three layers: what is missing that a document of this kind must carry, sentences that can be read two ways, and figures, dates and names that contradict each other inside the document. Go point by point — where it is, what is wrong, what it should say instead — ordered by what costs the most if left. If a layer turns up nothing, say so plainly; do not manufacture findings to fill all three. | search
- A manual somebody can actually follow | Write me a user manual. Read or look at the real thing I point you at first — do not write from an assumption about how it probably works. Write for somebody meeting this for the first time who knows none of the jargon. Order it the way a person actually does it, from the first step, one action per step, and for each step say what they should see if they got it right, so they know whether they are lost. Close with the mistakes people make most often and how to recover. Hand it back as a .docx with a table of contents. | package
- A formal letter that does not cause offence | Draft this letter for me. Ask first who it goes to, their position, what my relationship to them is, what I want them to do after reading it, and whether there is a deadline. Keep it to one page. Say the subject and the ask in the first paragraph — no circling. Pitch the register to the relationship: not so formal it reads as distant, not so casual it reads as rude. Hand it back as a .docx, and add two lines on how to follow up if they do not reply in time. | pencil
- What a document like this has to contain | For the kind of document I am about to produce, what does it have to contain? Do not draft anything yet — answer as knowledge first. Tell me what is mandatory, what is not mandatory but looks unprofessional to omit, and which parts people get wrong often enough to cause real trouble. If there is a legal requirement or a standard you can cite, say what you are citing and check on the web that it is still the current version. Where you are not sure, say you are not sure. Once you have answered, then ask me whether I want it drafted. | messageSquare
