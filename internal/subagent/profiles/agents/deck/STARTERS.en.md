# What are we presenting?

- A deck, built from a document I already have | Build me a deck from this file. Read the source through before outlining anything — do not convert it heading by heading, because the order that reads well on a page and the order that lands when spoken are not the same order. One idea per slide. Use only figures that appear in the file; invent nothing. Where a number matters, make it big enough to read from the back of the room rather than burying it in a bullet with everything else. Close on a slide saying what the audience has to decide. Hand it back as a .pptx and tell me which part of the source each slide came from. | fileText
- A deck, built from a recording of me talking | Build me a deck from this recording. Transcribe the whole file first, then work out what conclusion the speaker is walking the listener toward, and structure the deck around that conclusion rather than the order it was said in — people talking live circle back. Where a sentence is well put, keep the original wording rather than rewriting it into something flatter. Where the audio is unclear, mark it rather than guessing the word. Hand it back as a .pptx with the timestamp against each slide, and tell me what was said in the recording that did not make it onto a slide, and why. | headphones
- The structure, before any slides get built | Outline it for me first — do not build slides yet. Ask me who I am speaking to, how much they already know, how many minutes I have, and what I want them to do afterwards. Then propose a slide order with one sentence per slide saying what that slide is doing in the argument. Point out where the audience either believes us or does not, and what evidence has to sit at that point. If this would work better as a one-page document than as a deck, say so plainly, and then ask whether I still want slides. | layoutList
- A sales deck with enough weight to close | Build me a sales deck. Ask me first who it is for, what they are struggling with right now, how they currently deal with that, and how we differ from what they already do. Open on their problem, not on our history. Any customer name or result you cite must come from the real files I gave you — do not invent a figure or a logo. Put the price in the deck rather than hiding it at the end. Close on a specific next step with a date. Hand it back as a .pptx and tell me which slide is the one where the deal is actually lost if it does not land. | package
- Cut down to the time I actually have | I have limited time to speak — cut it to fit. Work from a real speaking pace of roughly a minute and a half per slide that carries content, and leave room for questions if there will be any. Tell me how many minutes over it currently runs before you propose anything. Then sort the cuts three ways: what can go with no damage to the argument, what can be merged, and what cannot go without the argument breaking. Do not simply drop the last few slides — cut by weight in the story. Move whatever comes out into an appendix at the back so it is there if somebody asks. | timer
- Where the audience stops following | Read this deck and tell me where the audience loses the thread. Go slide by slide as somebody who has never seen this material. Look for three things: a jump that skips a step, a piece of jargon used for the first time without explanation, and a slide carrying more than anyone can absorb at once. Give me slide numbers, why it breaks down there, and how to fix it without adding time. Close with the single change that would do the most good on its own. If the deck flows well, say it flows well rather than finding something to criticise. | eye
- A speaking script that matches the slides | Write me a speaking script for this deck. Write it as speech — short sentences, no nested clauses, nothing that sounds like reading aloud. Give each slide a rough number of seconds. Write the linking sentences between slides too, because the join is where people stumble. Where I should pause and let something land, write the pause in. Add short answers to the three questions the audience is most likely to ask at the end. Put it in the speaker notes of the same .pptx rather than a separate file. | messageSquare
- Two versions: one to present, one to send | Give me this deck in two versions. The first is for standing up and presenting: as little text on screen as possible, only the words that have to be seen, everything else moved into the speaker notes. The second is for sending to someone to read alone, which means putting the context I would normally say out loud back onto the slides so it stands up without a presenter. The two must say the same things — the read-alone version must not contain anything the spoken one does not. Hand back two clearly named .pptx files and tell me what the read-alone version had to add. | monitor
